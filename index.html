01 / FIELD GUIDE

Meet the jellyfish.

Moon jelly

Known for its translucent bell and four horseshoe-shaped reproductive structures visible through its body.

Sea nettle

A group of jellyfish with trailing tentacles that capture prey using specialized stinging cells.

Immortal jellyfish

Turritopsis dohrnii can, under certain conditions, revert from its adult medusa stage to an earlier polyp stage.

02 / GENETICS LAB

Instructions for life.

Genes contain instructions that cells use to build proteins. Some jellyfish proteins have become valuable tools in biological research.

Green fluorescent protein

GFP, first isolated from the jellyfish Aequorea victoria, can glow green under suitable illumination and helps researchers track biological processes.

Traits and genes

Traits arise through interactions among genes, cells, and the environment. One gene does not necessarily explain an entire complex trait.

Life-cycle changes

Some jellyfish have unusual life cycles. Scientists study how their cells change state and develop into different forms.

04 / BIO-INSPIRED ENGINEERING

Ideas from the ocean.

Soft robotics

Flexible, soft-bodied marine animals inspire researchers designing robots that can move and interact gently with delicate environments.

Efficient movement

Jellyfish propulsion provides ideas for studying fluid movement and designing underwater devices.

New questions

Biomimicry begins by observing a natural feature, understanding how it works, and asking whether its principles can solve a human design problem.
